## Onboarding

1. Register with your email, or accept an invitation, and enter the one-time
   code from your email.
2. Complete your profile if asked.
3. Choose your paths: **Data Room & Scoring**, **Ambassador**, **Apply for
   Residency**. Combine them as you like; each asks for its own consent.
4. Arrive at the cabinet.

You start as an Ambassador with 30 reputation points (40 plus a vouch gift if you
were invited), Anonymous trust level and Member tier. What you can do next
depends on the paths you add. See
[User roles and statuses](02-user-roles-and-statuses.md).

## Open a Data Room

The founder journey: present your company, get scored, be found.

1. **Create the room.** Anyone onboarded can create a Data Room for their own
   company; the first one is free. A Resident can also create a room about
   someone else's company. The room belongs to you, its creator.
2. **Upload documents** such as financials, legal, cap table and reports. Use
   PDF for anything you want recipients to open. Your first upload to a room of
   your own company switches on your top-up and scoring functions.
3. **Grant access** to named members. You can also invite teammates as Owner,
   Editor or Member. Any link to your room doubles as a referral link; see
   [Silent Data Room referrals](11-silent-dr-referral.md).
4. **Order a scoring.** The scoring rates the project on **Potential** (0-150)
   and **Readiness** (0-150). It needs a Pro room and costs $35, or one scoring
   voucher. See [Data Rooms, scoring and subscriptions](12-data-rooms-scoring-and-subscriptions.md).
5. **Publish to the project catalog.** A room appears in the public catalog with
   a valid scoring, a completed company profile and a Pro room. Without them the
   room still works for investors you granted access to.
6. **Close the room** when you are done: archive it (investors keep read access,
   the subscription pauses) or delete it permanently. You can also hand a room to
   another member, who must accept.

## Browse the dealflow

Open to Residents. Ambassadors can look at the Secondary Market and the Demand
Board read-only.

1. **Open the Dealflow.** Each card is a safe preview of a block a member placed.
2. **Open a block** to see the preview and the terms the seller chose to show.
   Seller contact details are never shown.
3. **Request access.** The seller and the operator review the request.
4. **Express interest** once access is approved. This starts a mediated
   introduction and opens a deal thread.

---

## Place a sale block

For the founder of record of a company whose own Pro Data Room holds a valid
scoring, and who is at least **Vouched** (two guarantees from Verified members)
or **Verified** (KYC passed). Anonymous members cannot place blocks.

1. Open the Seller Dashboard and create a block. It stays a draft until you
   submit it.
2. Submit it. There is no operator approval step: the block goes live at once,
   on a 72-hour freshness window for Verified sellers or a 24-hour window for
   Vouched sellers. The operator can take a block down afterwards if there is a
   complaint or a dispute.
3. Renew the block before its window closes, or it goes stale and then expires.
   This keeps the dealflow current.
4. Review access requests. Approved requests move to a mediated introduction.

## Vouch for a member

Backing a candidate on the waitlist. The first vouch needs a voucher with
verified KYC; the decisive second vouch comes from a Resident, KYC or not
_(rolling out; for now that Resident must have passed KYC too)_.

1. A candidate asks for your vouch.
2. You give it. They gain +10 reputation, you gain +5, and part of your own
   reputation is put at stake.
3. When the candidate holds two qualifying vouches, their Resident status
   completes.
4. If you later withdraw the vouch because they broke the rules, the stake is
   lost and a penalty applies. Only back people you can stand behind. See
   [Reputation economy](05-reputation-economy.md#backing-someone).

## Run a deal thread

1. After access is approved, a mediated introduction creates a private thread
   between buyer and seller. Find it under **Deals** and **Threads**.
2. Exchange messages and request materials. Names stay hidden until both sides
   confirm what they are willing to disclose. Keep the conversation in the
   thread: messages with direct contact details are held for operator review
   until the contract is signed.
3. The thread reaches an outcome and is archived. If something goes wrong, file a
   complaint or open a dispute from the thread; it stays open while the operator
   looks into it.

GBlock does not settle transactions, move funds or hold assets. The thread only
mediates the introduction and the conversation.

## Send an invitation

1. Open **Send Invite** and enter the person's email. **10 of your reputation
   points** are reserved as a stake.
2. They receive a secure link, onboard and start with 40 points and a vouch gift.
3. Your stake returns when the invitation activates or expires, at most 30 days
   later. If the invitee misbehaves, you may face a reputation impact.

Referral rewards appear under **Referrals** in the cabinet. See
[Payouts and rewards](10-payouts-and-rewards.md).

## Request a payout

USDT withdrawal opens later. At launch the wallet shows "Withdrawal opens later"
instead of a request form; your rewards stay in the reward balance and can be
moved into the payment balance. When it opens, there is no self-service
withdrawal: every payout is a reviewed request.

1. In the wallet, submit a payout request: at least **$50**, to a **USDT
   (BEP-20)** address. One request per calendar month. Without KYC the limit is
   $150 per 30 days; with KYC up to $1,500 per month _(rolling out)_.
2. The operator reviews it: first response within one business day, approved
   payouts sent within 20 business days.
3. You receive a notification with the transaction hash. A rejected request
   leaves your balance untouched and shows the reason.

Details: [Payouts and rewards](10-payouts-and-rewards.md).

## Report a problem

1. Open **Report** in the cabinet and pick a category: KYB / KYC,
   Misrepresentation, Off-platform, Harassment, Privacy, Fraud or Other.
2. Write to the operator at the address shown, with identifiers, dates and
   context. Evidence stays internal and your identity is not shown to the person
   you report.
3. The operator reviews and may warn, restrict, revoke a vouch or guarantee,
   remove a block or adjust reputation.

See [Trust and safety](07-trust-and-safety.md).
